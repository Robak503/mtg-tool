The Six Laws

  1. 1
    Glass needs light behind it.

    Every page sits on the aurora ground — soft mint/teal orbs on near-black. Frosted panels are invisible over flat darkness; the blur must have something to chew.

  2. 2
    One accent. Green is the star.

    Phosphor green carries identity; teal is the only sibling (secondary profiles, second data series). Amber and red exist solely as semantic states — warning and stop — never as decoration.

  3. 3
    Peer items dress identically.

    Deck cards, rows, tiles of the same kind share one finish. Data (names, pips, numbers) differentiates; decoration never does. A color difference must MEAN something.

  4. 4
    The page tells on itself.

    Every screen renders its own freshness — server version, registry identity, fetch age. Any mismatch flips the green box amber and disables launch. Stale can render; it cannot pretend.

  5. 5
    Desktop spreads, phone stacks — same parts.

    One component tree, two arrangements: rails around a canvas on desktop; glance-first stack with a fixed thumb dock on phone. Very like, never mirrors.

  6. 6
    Motion is transform and opacity only.

    Stripes slide, sheens sweep, buttons lift — all on the compositor. Never animate blur, layout, or color; respect prefers-reduced-motion always.
