# 1. WHAT SPECIAL ACTIONS ARE

Special actions (rule 116) are a third category of game action alongside spells/abilities and automatic game actions:

| Category | Uses stack? | Generated by | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spells and abilities | Yes | Players with priority | Casting spells, activating abilities |
| Turn-based actions | No | Game automatically | Draw step draw, untap, damage removal |
| State-based actions | No | Game automatically | Creature dying from lethal damage |
| **Special actions** | **No** | **Players with priority** | Land play, face-up turn, companion |

Special actions are player-initiated but stackless. They cannot be responded to with instants or abilities before they take effect because they don't create a stack object. They happen immediately when the player announces them.

# 2. THE TWELVE SPECIAL ACTIONS

## 2.1 Land Play (116.2a)

The most common special action. Timing requirements (all must be met):
- Player must have priority
- Stack must be empty
- Must be a main phase of that player's turn
- Player must not have already played a land this turn (unless an effect grants additional land plays)

Land play puts the land directly onto the battlefield from whatever zone it's in — normally the hand. It does not go on the stack, cannot be countered, and opponents cannot respond before it enters.

**Key distinction from casting:** Playing a land is not casting a spell. It cannot be countered by Counterspell. Effects that trigger "when a spell is cast" do not trigger. Effects that trigger "when a land enters the battlefield" do trigger.

## 2.2 Face-Down Creature Face Up (116.2b)

Turning a face-down permanent (typically a morph) face up. Can be done any time the player has priority — no stack-empty or main-phase requirement. The face-up action itself does not use the stack; any triggered abilities that fire when the creature turns face up go onto the stack normally.

## 2.3 Delayed-Effect Actions (116.2c)

Some effects give a player a future window to take an action — usually to end a continuous effect or stop a delayed trigger. These are special actions. Timing follows whatever restriction the effect specifies, defaulting to "any time with priority."

## 2.4 Static-Effect Ignorance (116.2d)

Some static abilities allow a player to opt out of the effect for a duration. Doing so is a special action, taken any time with priority.

## 2.5 Circling Vultures (116.2e)

A specific card-text special action allowing discard at instant speed. Narrow but confirmed as a special action category.

## 2.6 Suspend Exile (116.2f)

Exiling a suspend card from hand. Requires priority and that the player could otherwise begin to cast that card. The exile itself is the special action; the card then gains time counters per its suspend text.

## 2.7 Companion Fetch (116.2g)

Paying {3} to put a chosen companion from outside the game into hand. Timing: priority + empty stack + main phase of that player's turn + once per game per companion.

## 2.8 Foretell (116.2h)

Paying {2} to exile a card face down from hand for future casting at its foretell cost. Any time with priority during that player's turn. No stack-empty requirement.

## 2.9 Planar Die Roll (116.2i — Planechase only)

Rolling the planar die. Timing: priority + empty stack + main phase. Cost increases by the number of prior rolls that turn.

## 2.10 Conspiracy Face-Up (116.2j — Conspiracy Draft only)

Turning a face-down conspiracy in the command zone face up. Any time with priority.

## 2.11 Plot Exile (116.2k)

Exiling a plot card from hand for future casting without cost on a later turn. Any time with priority during that player's own turn + empty stack.

## 2.12 Unlock Cost (116.2m)

Paying the mana cost of a locked half of a Station card to unlock it. Timing: priority + empty stack + main phase of that player's turn.

# 4. PRIORITY AFTER SPECIAL ACTIONS (116.3)

After a player takes a special action, that player receives priority. This is the same result as after a spell resolves or an ability resolves — the active player gets priority if the special action was taken during priority sequencing.

**Engine consequence:** A special action does not pass priority. The player who took the action retains priority after it completes. Opponents then get the opportunity to respond (cast instants, activate abilities) only after the player who took the special action passes.

---

# 5. WHAT SPECIAL ACTIONS ARE NOT

**Not spells:** Cannot be countered by counterspells. Don't trigger "when a spell is cast" abilities.

**Not activated abilities:** Cannot be countered by ability counters (e.g., Stifle). Don't trigger "when an ability is activated" effects.

**Not turn-based actions:** Not generated automatically by the game. Player must choose to take them.

**Not state-based actions:** Not performed as corrections. They are intentional player decisions.

**No window to respond:** Since special actions don't use the stack, the action takes effect immediately. Opponents cannot cast instants "in response to" a land being played.

---

# 6. CROSS-LAYER CONNECTIONS

**→ Layer 2 (Time):** Land play (116.2a) is the special action that rule 505.6b describes — it can only occur during a main phase with empty stack. The L2 turn structure establishes when this window exists.

**→ Layer 6 (Priority, rule 117):** Rule 116.3 feeds directly into the priority model. After a special action, priority passes back per 117.3b. This is documented in L06_PlayerAction_117_t.md.

**→ Layer 7 (Object Model, rule 305):** Land play moves a land card to the battlefield. The ETB rules for lands (rule 305) govern what happens when it arrives. Zone-change triggers fire normally.
